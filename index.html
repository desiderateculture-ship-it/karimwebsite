He knows exactly what it's like when your body won't do what your mind knows.

Teachers segregated him from his class. He grew up believing he wasn't smart, and carried that belief into his business and his relationships, where it quietly cost him both.

Fixing it didn't come from trying harder. It came from going underneath — to the subconscious pattern running the show. That work changed his business, his marriage, and his life as a father.

The stutter was never in his mouth. It was in his mind. Yours isn't in your legs either.

07 · Straight Answers

The questions everyone asks about hypnotherapy.

What is hypnotherapy? How does it work?

Hypnotherapy uses relaxation and focus to help you work on habits, emotions, or challenges. When you're deeply relaxed, your mind is more open to positive changes.

Will I lose control during hypnosis?

No, you'll stay in control the whole time. You'll feel relaxed, but you can hear and respond to everything, and you won't do anything you don't want to.

What happens during a session?

We'll start by talking about what you want to work on. Then Karim guides you into a calm, relaxed state and gives you helpful suggestions to support the changes you're looking for.

Is hypnotherapy safe?

Yes, it's completely safe. Hypnosis is a natural state of relaxation that you've likely experienced before, like when you're daydreaming or really focused on something.

What does being hypnotised feel like?

It feels like a peaceful, daydream-like state. You're fully aware but super relaxed, and you might feel a little lighter or calmer afterward.

Will I remember what happens?

Yes, you'll remember everything we talk about. Hypnosis isn't sleep — it's more like a relaxed, focused state.

How do I know if this will work for me?

If you're open to the process and ready to make changes, it can work well. We'll discuss your goals upfront and take it from there.

Is this therapy?

No. This is performance work, not therapy. Karim Boktor is a mental performance coach, not a psychologist or medical practitioner. If you're in acute crisis, reach out to a crisis service or your GP first — this work complements professional care, it doesn't replace it.
